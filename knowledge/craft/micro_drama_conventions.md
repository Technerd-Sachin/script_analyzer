Micro-drama conventions (vertical, Indian market):

- **Format.** Vertical 9:16, shot for phones. Episodes run 1-2 minutes, about one page each. Frames are tight close-ups, so faces and reactions carry the scene and lip-sync is visible.
- **Hook first.** The first 5 seconds decide whether the viewer stays. Open on conflict, a question or a shock, never on routine or greetings.
- **Every episode ends on a hook.** Each episode closes on a cliffhanger: a reveal, threat, reversal or arrival. A resolved ending loses the viewer.
- **Paywall.** The first few episodes are free and a paywall follows episode N (set per show). The episode just before the paywall needs the strongest hook.
- **Fast escalation.** A new turn every 10 to 20 seconds. No subplots that cannot pay off within a few episodes.
- **Melodrama is expected.** Indian micro-drama borrows from TV serials: heightened emotion, clear villains, slaps and confrontations, family honour (izzat), wedding and in-law settings, secret identities, revenge and rich-poor romance. Judge it on clarity and punch, not realism.
- **Dialogue economy.** One or two short sentences per line. Subtext over explanation. Natural Hinglish code-mixing in urban stories.
- **On-screen text.** Phone messages, chats and captions often drive the plot and need graphics localization.
- **Characters.** Few speaking roles, archetypes made specific quickly: the wronged heroine, the cold rich hero, the scheming in-law.
